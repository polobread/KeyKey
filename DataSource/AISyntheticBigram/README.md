# AI synthetic bigram corpus

This directory contains small, reproducible bootstrap corpora for the macOS
Smart Mandarin language model. The sentences were generated by OpenAI Codex on
2026-09-23 from `generation-prompt.txt`, `generation-prompt-v2.txt`,
`generation-prompt-v3.txt`, and the public bug scenario supplied for this task.
No external web corpus or private document corpus was used.

`corpus-v1.txt`, `corpus-v2.txt`, and `corpus-v3.txt` are UTF-8 text with one
sentence per line. Spaces are explicit word boundaries. The cooker validates
each word against the McBopomofo reading tables and segments an unknown field
with the existing unigram model. Lines beginning with `#` are comments.

The first file contains 180 directly generated sentences. The second adds
exactly 1,000 unique sentences across 40 domains. Codex authored its sentence
parts, and `generate-corpus-v2.py` combines five beginnings with five endings
per domain, checks the 8–30 Han-character limit, rejects duplicates, and writes
the checked corpus deterministically. `generate-corpus-v3.py` adds 10,000
interleaved modal and situational variants, checks the 10–45 Han-character
limit, and rejects duplicates across the complete 11,180-sentence set.

This is a bootstrap model for engineering and A/B testing. It is intentionally
small and must not be described as a representative Taiwanese corpus. Future
versions can replace this file with a larger generated corpus without changing
the database format or the runtime input method.

Together the files contain 11,180 unique sentences. With the vendored
McBopomofo data they produce 131,838 segmented tokens and 6,236 SQLite bigram
rows after reading expansion. Set `SMART_BIGRAM_CORPORA=` when running
DatabaseCooker to build the unigram-only control database.

The cooker uses a unigram prior strength of 1,000. Because template repetition
is not observed language frequency, every distinct text bigram contributes at
most one synthetic observation. Repeating a prefix 5 or 50 times therefore has
the same model weight as including it once; additional sentences help only when
they introduce a new adjacent-word pair. The current build clips 138,946
repeated observations and retains 4,072 distinct text bigrams before reading
expansion.

## Synthetic article snapshots

`build-article-corpora.py` converts the accepted article bodies in
`../AISyntheticArticles/typing-articles-v2.jsonl` into cumulative 100-, 200-,
300-, 400-, 450-, 500-, 700-, 900-, 1,100-, 1,300-, 1,500-, and 1,650-article
corpora. It omits Markdown-only lines, preserves natural body lines and
punctuation, verifies that article IDs 1 through 1,650 are contiguous,
and writes hashes and source statistics to `article-corpus-build-report.json`.

`analyze-article-bigram-growth.py` runs the production cooker against the
existing bootstrap corpora plus each article snapshot. It builds temporary
SQLite databases, compares text-pair and reading-expanded row growth, and uses
articles 201–300 and 1,551–1,650 as held-out text for earlier models.
Results are written to `article-bigram-growth-report.json` and `.md`.

```sh
python3 DataSource/AISyntheticBigram/build-article-corpora.py
python3 DataSource/AISyntheticBigram/analyze-article-bigram-growth.py
python3 DataSource/AISyntheticBigram/build-filtered-article-corpora.py
python3 DataSource/AISyntheticBigram/analyze-corpus-filtering.py
python3 DataSource/AISyntheticBigram/analyze-ai-lexicon-impact.py
```

The measured 1,650-article snapshot adds 416,642 distinct text bigrams and
668,529 reading-expanded SQLite rows, increasing the minimal language-model
database by 33.45 MiB over the bootstrap model. Moving from 500 to 700 articles
adds 53,014 distinct text pairs, 84,480 reading-expanded rows, and 4.27 MiB.
Coverage of the held-out 1,551–1,650 block rises from 39.03% at 500 articles to
44.27% at 700 and 55.61% at 1,500.

Marginal yield declines from 265.1 new text pairs per article in the 501–700
block to 187.5 in the 1,501–1,650 block, so the corpus is showing diminishing
returns but is not saturated. A power-law fit over the six blocks from 501 to
1,650 has log-space R² 0.962 and projects roughly 2,982 articles before yield
falls below 150 pairs per article, 5,116 before 125, and 9,903 before 100.
These extrapolations are planning estimates; a different topic mix or pruning
policy can change the curve.

`build-filtered-article-corpora.py` and `analyze-corpus-filtering.py` compare
raw text with exact and conservative near-duplicate paragraph filtering. Exact
normalization removes 5,709 of 46,749 lines, and near deduplication removes 37
more. The near-deduplicated database retains 99.9993% of text pairs and exactly
the same 55.61% held-out coverage, but all three SQLite files occupy the same
41.30 MiB because the cooker already caps each synthetic pair at one and the
small row difference does not release an SQLite page. Paragraph deduplication
is useful for corpus hygiene but does not materially control the shipping
database size.

Held-out lexical-pair coverage on articles 201–300 rises from 1.27% at baseline
to 19.35% after 100 articles and 27.42% after 200 articles. Results that include
a held-out block itself are marked in-sample in the generated report.

## Versioned article corpus in the default build

`build-versioned-article-corpus.py` combines the validated v2 1,650, v3 350,
and v4 300 article exports in that order. It keeps each distinct body line once
after removing Markdown prefixes. Exact line matching preserves punctuation
and word boundaries; the cooker still caps every resulting text pair at one
observation. The output is `article-corpus-2300-exact-dedup.txt`, with source
hashes and removal counts in `article-corpus-2300-build-report.json`.

The DatabaseCooker Makefile regenerates this corpus when any article export
changes and includes it in `SMART_BIGRAM_CORPORA` by default. To rebuild and
measure the incremental v3 and v4 contribution:

```sh
python3 DataSource/AISyntheticBigram/build-versioned-article-corpus.py
python3 DataSource/AISyntheticBigram/analyze-versioned-article-impact.py
make -C Source/Distributions/Takao/DatabaseCooker all
```

The impact report is written to `versioned-article-bigram-impact.json` and
`.md`. Under identical vocabulary and bootstrap conditions, v3 adds 62,247
distinct text pairs and 4.88 MiB to the minimal language-model database;
v4 adds another 76,971 pairs and 6.08 MiB. The rebuilt full local
`CookedDatabase/KeyKey.db` contains 882,666 bigram rows and 559,913 distinct
text pairs, passes `PRAGMA integrity_check`, and occupies 63.94 MiB. An existing
installer package must be rebuilt separately to include that database.

## Number and unit phrases

`numeric-unit-lexicon.tsv` enumerates 1,157 Traditional Chinese number and
unit combinations for the default Smart Mandarin lexicon: common numerals
with 90 classifiers and units, selected zero-quantity forms, place values,
and formal financial numerals. Existing McBopomofo counts are retained when
higher; new combinations use low counts except for a few typing examples.
The separate file keeps the enumeration inspectable without changing input
method ranking code. `corpus-typing-feedback.txt` supplies financial-number
contexts for adjacent-word scoring.

Arabic-digit prefixes cannot be learned through this data path. The cooker
extracts Han runs from corpus text, and the associated-phrase collection
requires a Han first character. Entries such as `2篇` would therefore be
inactive without a runtime change; this data-only trial covers `二篇` and
`兩篇` instead.

## Categorized AI lexicon experiment

`analyze-ai-lexicon-impact.py` compares the current McBopomofo vocabulary plus
the project supplemental lexicon against the anime collection, the other 28
categorized collections, and all 29 collections. Every variant uses the same
bootstrap, typing-feedback, and article corpora. It also compares direct count
merging with `--lexicon-preserve-counts`, which adds words and readings without
raising an existing McBopomofo count.

All 29 collections add 4,806 words to the current Smart Mandarin vocabulary,
1,134 text pairs, 800 lost text pairs after resegmentation, and 0.41 MiB. On
held-out articles 1,551–1,650, lexical-pair coverage changes from 55.615% to
55.552%, a decrease of 0.062 percentage points. Preserving existing counts and
direct merging produce identical results here, so the change comes from added
words or readings rather than count replacement.

Anime alone adds 2,197 words but only 43 appear as surface strings in the 1,650
general typing articles. It adds 149 text pairs, loses 102 through
resegmentation, grows the database by 0.18 MiB, and changes held-out coverage by
-0.008 percentage points. Several entries are incomplete work-title fragments
or ordinary multiword strings, which can collapse useful general pairs such as
`約／成`, `借／物`, `物／主`, and `任務／完成`. Keep anime opt-in unless the
collection receives a dedicated proper-name and fragment review. The other 28
collections contain 2,616 new loadable words, but only 104 occur in the article
corpus; review those observed terms as a compact allowlist before considering a
core Smart Mandarin merge. Full results are in `ai-lexicon-impact-report.md`.

The cooker reads the first three columns from categorized TSV files and ignores
their category or source columns. Mixed-script readings such as `A`, `AI`, and
`1/2` cannot be encoded as Bopomofo qstrings; 18 such rows are skipped and remain
available only through the associated-phrase system.

The earlier 100–1,650 article snapshots remain experiments. The combined
2,300-article corpus is included in `SMART_BIGRAM_CORPORA` by default. Pair
coverage and local score lift do not replace end-to-end candidate-order A/B
testing.
